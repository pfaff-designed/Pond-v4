what it does

A pond is a workspace with its own pages, databases, memory, threads and change log. A shared pond is a pond many people belong to, and each person sees only what is shared with them. Comments, live editing, clashes and removing someone all run through diffs and records.

create a pond, switch between ponds, and manage them6m · storyboard 13
1
open the switcherThe pond’s name sits at the top of the rail. Clicking it lists every pond, with ⌘1, ⌘2 and ⌘3 to jump straight to one.
one placethe switcher is the rail header. Nothing else in the rail changes until you pick.
2
name, glyph, themeA short version of onboarding. The name is typed; the glyph and theme have defaults, and each pond keeps its own theme.
skippableskip gives “untitled pond”, the first glyph and the paper theme.
3
areasSuggested areas, all optional. Each one picked becomes an empty page under the new pond’s root.
no meaningan area is only a page with pages inside. Picking none is fine.
4
sourcesConnect outside data now or later. Whatever is connected belongs to this pond only.
per ponda source connected here never shows up in life.
5
the new pondThe shell opens on the new pond’s root page. The areas are empty pages; the composer reads only this pond.
separatepages, databases, memory, threads and the change log are per pond. Nothing crosses over.
6
or from ⌘KPonds are listed in the palette too, above pages. ↵ switches; the page you left stays open for when you come back.
both waysswitcher and ⌘K reach the same place. Neither is the main one.
7
manage pondsSettings lists every pond. Drag to reorder; the menu renames, changes the glyph and theme, exports, archives or deletes.
archivean archived pond is read-only and leaves the switcher. restore it from archived here.
deleteyou type the pond’s name to confirm. it waits 30 days under deleted here, then goes.
sharedanyone can leave a shared pond. only its last admin can delete it.
exporta whole pond exports as files, pages and databases together.
8
mobileThe same switcher, at thumb height, and the same three steps as sheets.
same as desktopevery pond action is on mobile too.
belong to a shared pond, see only what’s shared with you, share a page, and propose edits to someone else’s page6n · storyboard 14
1
your ponds and shared pondsThe switcher lists your own ponds, then shared ponds you belong to, then single pages people have shared with you. Your own ponds stay yours; nobody in studio collective can see life.
shared pondone pond that many people belong to. It has its own pages, databases, memory and change log.
severalyou can belong to as many shared ponds as you’re invited to.
2
inside a shared pondSam sees only the pages shared with him. The rest of studio collective is not in his rail, his search or Pond’s answers. There is no hint that anything else exists.
the pondowns every page in it. The person who made a page is its author.
nesteda shared page brings the pages nested under it.
3
a new page is only yoursA page Sam makes in studio collective starts with only him able to see it, marked only you. It stays that way until he shares it.
authorSam is the author, so edits by anyone else come to him as diffs.
privateno one else can open, find or ask about it, admins included.
4
share an area or pageAna shares clients from the row’s ⋯ › who can see, the one place sharing happens. Each person has view, comment or edit. See 6w.
one placewho can see, in the row menu. There is no share dialog.
own pondthe same row menu in your own pond. Only people in the pond are listed.
5
people and rolesAn admin invites people to the pond and sets roles. Being a member lets people be shared with; it doesn’t open any page by itself.
moderatoraccepts, rejects or requests changes on any page shared with them at edit, not only their own, and can accept their own.
admineverything a moderator can do, plus people and roles. Can open every page in the pond except private ones; takes over the pages of anyone who leaves: the admin who removed them, or the longest-standing admin if they left. any admin can hand the pages on.
invitethe invite is the only email pond sends. the link opens sign-in with the address filled in and lands in the shared pond.
peopleeach row in settings › people has change role and remove.
membermakes pages, shares them, and reviews diffs on the pages they author.
guestinvited to the pond in settings › people; sees only what is shared with them. The only way in from outside.
6
an edit is a proposalEditing a page you are not the author of makes a proposed diff for its author, the pond’s moderators and admins. Your version shows only to you, marked pending, until one of them accepts, rejects or asks for changes.
author decidesonly the author’s edits apply straight away. A moderator’s edit to someone else’s page is still a diff.
7
the author or a moderator reviews itOn Ana’s side it arrives like any proposed diff, named with who sent it. Moderators see it too, on the page and in the change log. The change log records who sent it.
same buttonsaccept, reject, request changes. Diffs from people and diffs from Pond work the same way.
8
someone asks to joinSomeone outside the pond who opens a link sees no access and can ask. The request goes to the pond’s admins, who invite them in settings › people.
approvea link only ever lets someone ask.
9
linking to a shared pageIn his own pond, Sam can @ a page shared with him. The link shows which pond it lives in. When access ends the link stays, marked no access.
nothing keptending access copies nothing.
10
what your Pond readsSam’s Pond can read a shared page when his page links to it, and says so in the read chips. It records nothing from it into his memory, and his memory is never shared.
memoryyour memory notes come only from your own pond.
11
a shared pond’s memoryStudio collective has a memory of its own, recorded only from pages shared in it. Each note is shown only to people who can open its source.
only youpages marked only you never feed it, and neither do threads. Each person’s threads are private to them.
same rulesevery note is sourced, logged and deletable, by the author of its source or an admin.
12
mobileThe same switcher and the same shared pond. Sharing, accepting and requests all work on the phone.
same as desktopevery sharing action is on mobile too.
comment and resolve, share a page live, settle a clash, review across the pond, and remove someone cleanly6p · storyboard 16
1
leave a commentSelect a block and comment in the margin. The comment is anchored to that block and marks it with a count. It never changes the page.
a recordeach comment is a record in the pond’s comments database.
whoanyone the page is shared with, at edit or comment.
2
reply, ask, resolveReplies stack in the same margin card. Pond answers only when someone writes @pond, and its answer is a draft until a person keeps it. Resolve closes the card and clears the mark.
pondnever joins a comment on its own. Its draft shows what it read.
what it readsonly what everyone on the comment can open, so nothing leaks to someone without access.
resolvedstays in the comments database; nothing is deleted.
3
two on one pageWhen someone else has the page open, their initials sit on its row in the rail (6w), and that is all. Their typing becomes a proposed diff; the block shows that one is on its way.
quietinitials in the rail only. No cursors, no typing indicator.
every editby anyone but the author arrives as a diff. The author’s edits apply as they type.
whenedits to one block collect into one diff, sent when you leave the block. until then it shows as pending.
4
two edits, one blockSam and Ana both propose a change to final copy before either is accepted. Ana, the author, or any moderator sees them side by side and picks one, merges them, or rejects both.
nothing lostthe one not picked is marked outdated in the change log, with who sent it.
after accepta diff that arrives on a block already changed still goes outdated, as before.
5
mergeMerge asks Pond for one diff that keeps both intents. It lists both sources and goes through accept like any diff. Back to both returns to the choice.
a draftthe merge is Pond’s proposal. The author or a moderator can request changes to it.
6
review across the pondIn a shared pond, the change log’s proposed view is where review happens: every proposed diff on every page you can see, in one list, with the diff open beside it. Authors and moderators answer here or on the page.
moderatorcan accept, reject or request changes on any page shared with them at edit. Admins can too; authors always can on their own pages.
only yours to seethe list holds diffs on pages shared with you, nothing else.
namedeach accepted record says who accepted it.
7
Sam’s outdated diffOn Sam’s side, his edit is outdated and says why: Ana accepted a merge. He can revise it against the page as it is now, or withdraw it.
revisePond redrafts his edit on the current block; he sends it again.
his recordthe pond’s change log keeps what he sent.
8
access removed mid-sessionAna removes Sam from launch plan while he has it open. The page is replaced by a plain notice. He stays a member of studio collective and keeps any other page shared with him.
cleanopen diffs are withdrawn and logged as withdrawn on Ana’s side.
commentsstay on her page under his name.
9
comments and the one countComments open as a database like any other, with open and resolved views. The only signal that someone did something is the count on the change log: diffs you can review, on any page.
no pingsno badges, emails or pushes for comments or proposals.
@mentionsput the comment in that person’s proposed view. nothing is sent.
resharingonly the author, moderators and admins can reshare a page.
10
mobileThe same breadcrumb presence and the same comments, opened from a block. Replying, @pond and resolve all work on the phone.
same as desktopevery multi-user action is on mobile too.
